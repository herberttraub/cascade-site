A $100 billion problem, hidden in plain sight

U.S. merchants pay well over $100B a year to accept cards, priced off interchange tables that run to hundreds of categories and change twice a year. Every swipe stacks three fee layers — but the statement only ever shows the blended total, so a fair deal and an inflated one look identical.

How it works

One pipeline, five stages

Deterministic per-processor parsing; only the unrecognized long tail touches an LLM, capped at medium confidence. Everything downstream is arithmetic against cited tables.

1
Parse

Any processor's PDF → normalized line items, reconciled to the statement's own totals within $1. Unknown layouts go to human review.

2
Benchmark

Every charge measured against the published Visa/Mastercard tables and Reg II — each rate carrying its source doc, page, and hash.

3
Audit

Six checks — downgrades, padding, markup, routing, junk fees, Reg II cap — each a finding with evidence, citation, and math.

4
Report

A client-ready report where every finding shows the statement line, the published rate, and the full arithmetic.

5
Corpus

Every audit deposits an anonymized record — the benchmarking dataset that compounds with each statement.

Why you can trust it

Defensible by design

A finding only helps if it survives the processor pushing back — so everything is cited, conservative, and reproducible.

Processed locally

Statements are audited on our systems and never uploaded to third-party services. Merchant identifiers are stripped before anything enters the corpus.

Every finding is cited

Each carries the statement line, the exact published rate with document, page, and checksum, and the full math.

Never guessed

No rate in the tables? We emit “unknown” and flag it for a human. When the tier mix is unknown we benchmark conservatively — savings are understated by construction.

Why it compounds

Every audit makes the next one smarter

Each engagement deposits an anonymized record — processor, pricing model, effective rate, downgrade share, fee patterns. That corpus powers peer benchmarking (“your rate is higher than 78% of comparable merchants”) and gets harder to replicate with every statement.
